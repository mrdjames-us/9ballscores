About 9-Ball Scores

9-Ball Scores is a free scorekeeper for league-style 9-ball pool, built to run on a phone at the table. It keeps score in the handicapped points format many amateur leagues use: balls 1–8 are worth one point, the 9 is worth two, and each player races to the point total for their skill level. It also tracks defensive shots, innings, dead balls, 9 on the snap and break-and-runs, then works out the 20-point match split when the match ends.

No account or download is needed. Your matches are saved on your phone, and you can optionally sign in with Google or use a shared sync code to keep history on more than one device.

Start a match

  1. On the Score tab, pick each player from your saved players or type a name. Tick Guest for a one-off opponent you don't want in your history.
  2. Set each player's skill level. The points each player needs to win appear underneath.
  3. Choose who won the lag. That player breaks first and shoots first.
  4. Tap Start Match.

Keep score

  • Tap a ball when the shooter pockets it. The shooter's score and progress bar update right away.
  • Miss ends the turn. Defense marks a safety and ends the turn. Foul / Scratch ends the turn and gives ball in hand.
  • Dead Ball, then tap the ball(s), for balls that score for no one, such as balls made on a foul.
  • When the 9 drops, the app asks whether it was 9 on the snap, a break-and-run or an early 9, and starts the next rack with the right breaker.
  • Undo takes back the last action if you tap the wrong thing.

When a player reaches their points total, the app announces the winner and shows the match-point split. Choose Rematch to play again with the same players, or New Players to set up a new match.

Players and history

  • The Players tab saves regular players and their skill levels so setup takes seconds. Renaming a player updates their past matches too.
  • The History tab lists every finished match, with career stats per player: wins, points, defenses, snaps and break-and-runs. You can export your history as a file.
  • Sign in with Google to keep your own history across devices, or create a sync code to share one scorebook between phones without logging in.

Rules reference

The Rules tab summarizes the format the scorer follows: scoring, fouls, dead balls, innings, defensive shots, the points-to-win chart and the 20-point match chart. Your league's own rules and rulings always take priority.
